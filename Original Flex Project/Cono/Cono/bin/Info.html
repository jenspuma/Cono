Background

Raymond Queneau's Cent Mille Milliards de Poemes is derived from a set of ten core sonnets. In his book, published in 1961, the sonnets are printed on cardboard with each line on a separated strip like a heads-bodies-and-legs book. All ten sonnets have the same rhyme scheme and employ the same rhyme sounds. As a result, any line from a sonnet can be combined with any from the other nine giving 1014 (= 100,000,000,000,000) different poems.
Even if they lack narrative, each of the core sonnets has a theme: 1 - South America, 2 - ancient Greece, 3 - the sea, 4 - India, 5 - Italy, 6 - visiting Paris, 7 - twinhood, 8 - poetry, 9 - food and drink, 10 - death.

British professor Beverly Charles Rowe has translated the poems into English. He has gone to great lengths to stay true to the original preserving the rhyme sounds of the French text. Rowe has published several different interactive versions on his website. He has added two - in my opinion very beautiful - extra sonnets of his own. Their themes are global warming and fashion.

In 2006 I came across Rowe's interactive version and decided to put music to the poems. As it turned out it would be a while before I had the time and means to do so. In November 2011 I started writing and recording the music for the sonnets, but it would be another half year before I could dedicate some time to recording the vocals and writing the code. Considering how long this idea had been on its way I was quite a magical moment for me when the first prototype came to life in late November 2012. And now finally it is ready for the world to enjoy!

About this work

This work is based on Rowe's 2006 version and uses all 12 sonnets, hence the number of combinations amount to 1214 (=1,283,918,464,548,864). Since every sonnet has a playing time of 1:30 minutes it will take about 35 million centuries to listen to all combinations!

I have taken the liberty to change the text in one place, the last line of sonnet 4. Rowe's translation reads: "the shield of vair or or's but briefly seen" but after consulting the French original I decided to use the translation "the shield of vair or gold's but briefly seen", which in turn is more singable. Any other deviations from Rowe's text may be regarded as typos or "singos".

The music has been composed specifically for this work. It incorporates elements from both traditional folk music as well as contemporary electronic music. It starts out softly and gradually gains intensity. The first four verses employ instruments such as strings, oboe and flute, but after verse four it shifts towards a more electronic arrangement using synthesized sounds. Following the electronic section the music breaks down into a softer verse after which it loops ad infinitum.

Ideas for additional features and future improvements

French version
Since Rowe has stayed true to the rhyme sounds of the French original it would be possible to make a mixed English/French version of the song. The listener would then be allowed to choose between English or French for each line. In terms of programming this is not a big task, but it would require for someone to sing and record the French versions of the sonnets. If anybody knows of anyone who would be able to and interested in doing so, please contact me.

More variation in the music
Currently the underlying music is just a looped piece of linear music. But the music rendering engine, which I have created for this project, is capable of handling a lot more complexity in the music than what is required for this work. It would be interesting to explore the possibilities of varying the music dynamically.

Option to select a specific sonnet
A feature I would like to add is an option to select a specific sonnet with a single mouse click.

Pause button
Another handy feature would be a pause button.

Page turn effect
My original plan was to implement the switching between lines as a real page turn, similar to the effect used in online magazines and adds. I did a bit of research into the code required for this, but I decided to focus my effort on getting a minimal version up and running. Page turn effect would be nice, but it also works without it.

iOS version
As the current version is written in Flash, iOS device users will have to wait for the 100000000000000 Songs app.
